Take the RAW format you have and make a JPG

Five pages, one per format: what is inside a Nikon NEF, a Canon CR3/CR2, a Sony ARW, an Adobe or Apple DNG and a Fujifilm RAF, how the browser decodes it, and what happens when a frame cannot be unpacked.

Updated 2026-11-25

If you only want to know whether your file opens here: all five are supported — drop it into the editor; there is no format to choose first. Each page covers the container itself: what it holds, which bodies write it, and how the page handles a frame it cannot decode.

All five share one pipeline: a 16-bit decode that keeps four stops of scene headroom above white, scene-linear development on the GPU, darktable’s sigmoid display transform, and the 64 looks applied after that transform. The full explanation and the measured numbers are in opening a RAW in the browser.

    Convert a Sony ARW to JPG in the browser

    You have one ARW file and want a JPEG: drop it into the browser window, develop it and export. Decoding runs on your machine; the file is never uploaded and no account is needed.
