Four pairs that are easy to mix up

Kodak Portra 400 / Portra 160 / Gold 200

Portra 400 is the warm, steady one: red lifted slightly, blue pulled down, and red deepened only where the image already runs warm while the orange band around skin is held back. Portra 160 is the cleaner, more neutral sibling — less warmth, no red accent. Gold 200 is deliberately further out: a stronger gold cast and softer highlights; it never tries to be neutral.

Kodak Tri-X 400 / Fujifilm Acros 100

Both are monochrome, but the conversions differ. Tri-X uses a near-panchromatic mix with a firmer midtone curve and a lower black point, so it reads heavy. Acros gives green more weight in the greys, keeps red and blue smaller, and uses a gentler curve with a neutral black — which is why fog, snow and many-step gradients separate more easily on it.

Fujifilm Velvia 50 / Classic Chrome

Opposite directions. Velvia 50 is the most saturated recipe in the pack (a 1.28 saturation multiplier is baked in): deep greens, magenta-leaning shadows, high saturation. Classic Chrome pulls back — lower saturation, cooler shadows, moderate contrast — and suits documentary work. Want more flavour, take Velvia; want the image to step back, take Classic Chrome.

Cinema Teal / Bleach Bypass

Cinema Teal separates by hue: shadows pushed toward cyan, highlights kept warm, skin standing out because it sits in the orange highlight range. Bleach Bypass works the other way: it raises contrast and drops saturation so the bright areas go silver — it does not move hue separation, it drains colour and hardens the tone steps.

How to choose

  • People: Portra 400 (warm, stable skin), Hasselblad Natural (the most neutral, softest highlights), Leica Classic (warm, deep blacks, richer reds).
  • Landscape: Velvia 50 (dense greens, high saturation), Ektar 100 (vivid reds, cool shadows), Ricoh GR Positive (high contrast, strong reds and greens).
  • Monochrome: Tri-X 400 (heavy, firm), Acros 100 (fine, flat), and Acros's green weighting changes how foliage separates from sky.
  • Mood: Cinema Teal (cool shadows, warm highlights), Bleach Bypass (high contrast, low saturation), Kodachrome 64 (deep reds, solid blacks, cool shadows).

If you are unsure, open the editor, drop a photo in and sweep the filmstrip — everything renders locally, and what you see is the version the export produces.
