Kodak Portra 400: preview it in the browser, download the .cube

暖肤色、柔肩、阴影微冷. This page shows the actual result on one sample photo, what subjects it suits and how it differs from its two nearest looks — apply it in the editor or download this single .cube.

Updated 2026-11-253 min read

What Kodak Portra 400 is

A warm-leaning recipe: red is lifted slightly, blue pulled down, and red is deepened only where the image already runs warm, while the orange band around skin is held back so faces do not slide toward orange. Highlights begin rolling off softly past the middle, the shadows carry a trace of cool, and saturation stays close to the original.

What it suits

  • Overcast or window-lit street portraits
  • Backlit skin and pale clothing
  • Weddings and family groups where skin has to stay believable

How it differs from its two nearest looks

Kodak Portra 160 is the cleaner, more neutral version — less warmth, no red accent and a plainer highlight roll-off; Kodak Gold 200 goes much further into gold and softens the highlights more, where this one keeps the whites cleaner.

How to use it

Open it in the editor

  1. The button opens the editor with this look already applied (the photo is processed on your device, never uploaded).
  2. Want the file: download kodak-portra-400.cube (standard 33³, about 0.8 MB), or take all 64 as one pack.
  3. Elsewhere: Resolve → Project Settings → Color Management → Lookup Tables; Premiere → Lumetri → Creative → Look; Photoshop → Color Lookup adjustment layer. Lightroom Classic cannot open a .cube directly, but you can package it as a profile in Adobe Camera Raw (Presets ⋯ → Create Profile → tick Color Lookup Table) and use it from Lightroom’s Profile Browser.

The limits

An original recipe written for this site. It is inspired by the look of Kodak Portra 400; it is not film-scan data and not a vendor-published LUT. One photo at a time, no batch. Judge it at 100 % strength and lower it to taste; two looks can also be merged into a new .cube.
